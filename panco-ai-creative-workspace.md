Turning a short drama script into moving images starts with decisions about characters, locations, and visual style. The [Panco AI creation platform](https://panco.ai/) brings short drama creation, image generation, video generation, an infinite canvas, and asset management into one workspace, so creators can develop these parts within the same project.


This article is published by the Panco brand account and was prepared with AI assistance from the features and workflows shown on the official homepage on September 20, 2026. It is a product overview, not an independent review or a claim that every model capability has been tested.


## Start with an existing script and define the production tasks


The short drama workflow described on Panco's homepage begins with importing an existing script and confirming its episodes and production elements. Creators can then establish a visual direction around characters, scenes, styles, and key reference images.


For anyone preparing a short drama, this sequence separates two judgments: whether the story works and whether its visual treatment works. An attractive generated image cannot answer both questions. Before generating a scene, it helps to identify where it takes place, who appears in it, and what the shot needs to communicate.


That checklist is a creative suggestion, not an additional claim that Panco automatically reviews scripts, approves storyboards, or edits a complete film. The homepage presents a route from an existing script into production; it does not guarantee a release-ready film from a single sentence.


## AI image creation: establish the visual direction first


The image workflow presented on the homepage includes generation, editing, and reference-guided creation. Featured image model families include Seedream, Nano Banana, and GPT Image. The page also describes controls related to aspect ratio, composition, text rendering, and image editing. The available options depend on the model offered in the current workspace.


When using an [AI image creation platform](https://panco.ai/) to prepare short drama assets, a useful first task is one character or one location. Define its main features, keep a reference image you are happy with, and check whether the next image still fits that visual plan. Changing fewer variables makes it easier to identify whether a problem comes from the prompt, the reference, or the scene requirements.


For example, when preparing a wide shot and a close-up of the same interior, compare the furnishings, lighting direction, and character's clothing. This is an example of what to inspect, not a tested guarantee of character consistency.


## AI video creation: turn an approved image into a shot


Panco's homepage describes video creation from text, images, or reference material, with controls that depend on the selected model. Featured video model families include Seedance, HappyHorse, and Wan.


A practical starting point is to approve a still image and then specify the movement it needs. Is a character turning around? Is the camera moving closer? Does the scene need only a subtle change? A clear motion goal helps distinguish an image that fits the setting from a shot that serves the story.


Reference modes, audio support, duration, and output options can differ between models. A feature listed for one model should not be treated as a shared specification for every model. Check the current workspace for available settings and resource costs.


## Infinite canvas and asset management: keep useful context


The homepage presents the infinite canvas as a place to organize ideas and visual content. Asset management provides a way to save images, videos, and uploaded references, with folders for organizing material to reuse in later work.


For a project that needs several rounds of revision, consider grouping assets into references, approved images, and candidate video clips. These are suggested categories, not a description of preset folders. Recording what each group is for helps distinguish useful results from experiments and material that still needs another attempt.


Saving an output is only part of completing a project. Creators still need to assess character continuity, how shots connect, and whether each asset suits its final use.


## Try one small sequence before expanding the project


A short script excerpt or a clearly defined creative task can help you assess whether the workflow fits your needs:


- Prepare a script excerpt, a brief, and reference material you have permission to use.
- Define the characters, setting, and visual style, then generate key images.
- Choose a suitable image and try a video shot with a specific motion goal.
- Review the content, visual details, and movement, and save results worth reusing.
- Decide what to change based on those results before expanding the production.

These steps interpret the connected workflow shown on the homepage. They are not a button-by-button tutorial. The interface, model options, and output quality should be assessed in the product as it is available when you use it.
